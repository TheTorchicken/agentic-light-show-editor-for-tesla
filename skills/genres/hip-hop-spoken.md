# Hip-hop, rap and spoken word

A voice leading over beats: rap, spoken word, speeches over a track. Learned from a
spoken-word track over trap drums.

## Why these shows fail

The voice is the lead, but most tools light the drums and ignore the words. The first AI
version of this song moved every headlight channel as one block, on and off, the whole
time. The owner rated it "bad".

## How to choreograph it

1. **Light the phrases.** Find vocal phrases in the vocals stem (where its energy rises
   from silence and falls back). Give each phrase a gesture: the DRL "eyes" fade up on the
   phrase and down at its end, alternating sides phrase by phrase. A punchline or a key word
   gets a hit.
2. **The drums are a figure, not a grid.** Trap and boom-bap patterns repeat a figure every
   bar (e.g. hit A, then B 0.55 s later, then C). Map each hit of the figure to a different
   part of the car (A front, B rear, C both) so the pattern reads as a pattern.
3. **Sub-bass is the cabin.** Let the screen and accents swell with the bass stem's energy;
   it reads as weight.
4. **Spoken breaks go quiet.** When the beat drops out and only the voice is left, strip the
   exterior to the eyes and let the screen follow the voice's loudness.
5. **Drops and choruses** use the electronic skill's rules: darkness first, then everything.

## Check before you finish

- The DRL strips fade with the voice; they are not on as a background (the critique flags
  any light on more than 35% of the time).
- Left and right differ most of the time.
