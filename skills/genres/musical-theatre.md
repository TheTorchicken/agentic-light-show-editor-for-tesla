# Musical theatre and narrative songs

A cast telling a story: musicals, narrative ballads, songs with several characters and an
ensemble. Learned from Alexander Hamilton.

## How to choreograph it

1. **Cast the car.** Give each voice or character its own part of the car and keep it for
   the whole song: one narrator the left side, another the right, another the rear, the
   lead the headlights and the screen. The audience learns who is singing from where the
   light is.
2. **Follow the story, not the loudness.** Mark the narrative beats (introductions, turning
   points, the moment the title is sung, the roll call) and give each its own gesture.
   Loudness alone flattens a story.
3. **The ensemble is everything.** When the full company sings, the whole car moves: sides
   swap on the kick, both ends strobe, the screen goes full, the windows come down so the
   voices pour out. Save this; it should be the only time the car looks like this.
4. **Darkness before entrances.** A character's big entrance lands better after a beat of
   black.
5. **The finale is the biggest moment of the show**, then end on one clean final image.

## Check before you finish

- Each character's zone is consistent across the song.
- Nothing is on as a background through the verses (the first Hamilton draft left the DRL
  strips on 45% of the time; the critique flags it).
- The ensemble sections are the brightest in the show.
